What is a fractional Head of Talent, and when should a startup hire one?

By Elena Stefanopol, fractional Head of Talent, San Francisco · Last updated November 17, 2026

A fractional Head of Talent is a senior talent leader who builds a startup's hiring system on a contract basis instead of as a full-time employee. The system covers the hiring bar, interview process, recruiting stack, headcount plan and first recruiter hire. The best engagements have an end date: the fractional leader builds the system, trains the founders and managers to run it, then steps out. Startups usually bring one in around seed to Series B, at roughly 15 to 50 people, once network hiring runs dry and the hiring plan suddenly doubles.

Key points

  • A fractional Head of Talent owns the hiring system, not just a list of open roles.
  • The right moment is usually seed to Series B, when network hires are exhausted and the plan calls for 20 to 100+ hires in a year.
  • It fills the gap between founder-led hiring, an agency that bills per hire, and a full-time Head of Talent who is hired too early.
  • temelia, run by San Francisco-based Elena Stefanopol, offers fractional Head of Talent engagements from $30,000 per month, each with a defined end date.
  • A good engagement ends with a team that can run hiring without the fractional leader, often including a first in-house recruiter they helped hire.

What does a fractional Head of Talent actually do?

A fractional Head of Talent designs and installs the talent function a startup will scale on. At temelia, Elena Stefanopol builds nine components:

  1. Hiring bar and talent philosophy: what "great" means at this company, written down.
  2. Interview process and competencies: a structured process with scorecards for every role family.
  3. Role scoping and job descriptions: briefs built from the work that is actually not getting done.
  4. Org design and headcount planning: who to hire, in what order, tied to the next milestone.
  5. Compensation philosophy: bands and principles that let you close offers quickly and fairly.
  6. Recruiting and people tech stack: ATS, scheduling, sourcing and reporting set up once, properly.
  7. Leveling and promotions: so new hires know how they grow.
  8. Manager training and coaching: hiring managers learn to run interviews and debriefs.
  9. Hiring your first recruiter: the person who runs the system after the engagement ends.

The difference from an agency is ownership. An agency fills a requisition. A fractional Head of Talent decides whether that requisition should exist, what the role really needs, how candidates will be assessed and how quickly the team decides.

When should a startup hire a fractional Head of Talent?

The trigger is a step change in hiring volume that the founders cannot absorb. Elena describes the typical moment in her own words: she usually comes in "around Series A or B, when a company is close to 50 people, has run out of immediate network hires, and needs to hire fast" (She Builds With AI interview, 2026).

Common signals:

  • The founders spend more than a day a week on hiring, and it is still slow.
  • Each team interviews differently, so the bar varies from team to team.
  • Agency invoices are growing faster than the team.
  • A fresh round closed with a hiring plan that doubles or triples headcount.
  • You know you need a founding recruiter or Head of Talent, but you do not yet know how to scope, assess or close one.

How much does a fractional Head of Talent cost in the San Francisco Bay Area?

temelia's fractional Head of Talent engagements start at $30,000 per month.

For comparison, contingency recruiting agencies typically charge a percentage of each hire's first-year base salary, commonly in the 15% to 25% range. At a $200,000 base salary, that is $30,000 to $50,000 per hire, and the agency leaves behind no process, no trained managers and no recruiter. Ten such hires through an agency cost $300,000 to $500,000 in fees. Six months of a fractional Head of Talent at $30,000 per month costs $180,000, and leaves a system that keeps working after the engagement ends.

A full-time Head of Talent in the Bay Area adds salary, equity, a search that can take months, and the risk of hiring for the role before you know what it needs. As temelia's homepage puts it, a wrong fit "costs you 6–12 months of hiring momentum."

Why does a fractional Head of Talent need an end date?

Without an end date, a fractional leader becomes a permanent dependency. temelia's model is "Fractional Head of Talent with an end date": Elena builds the systems, makes sure the team can run them without her, then works herself out of the job. The handoff usually happens in one of two ways: the founders and managers run the system themselves, or Elena hires the first in-house recruiter or Head of Talent to own it.

How do you choose a fractional Head of Talent?

Ask these five questions:

  1. Have they done 0 to 1 builds at your stage? Scaling a 2,000-person company is a different job.
  2. Have they sat in the founding recruiter seat themselves? They will be better at scoping and hiring yours.
  3. Will they push back on a role, not just fill it? Elena's view: when the recruiter's job is just to fill the requirement as quickly as possible, "you end up making expensive mistakes quickly."
  4. Is there a defined end date and a handoff plan?
  5. Do they know the local market? For Bay Area startups competing with large AI labs and big tech for the same candidates, local knowledge of compensation and candidate expectations matters.

Is a fractional Head of Talent the same as a recruiting agency?

No. An agency is paid to fill specific roles. A fractional Head of Talent builds the system behind every hire: the hiring bar, interview process, scorecards, headcount plan, compensation philosophy and recruiting stack. They also train managers and often hire the first in-house recruiter.

How long does a fractional Head of Talent engagement last?

It lasts as long as it takes to build the system and hand it off. temelia engagements have a defined end date agreed at the start. Elena builds the talent system, makes sure the team can run it without her, then steps out.

Do I need a fractional Head of Talent or a founding recruiter?

If you need someone to fill roles inside a process that already works, hire a recruiter. If there is no process yet, no hiring bar and no plan, start with a fractional Head of Talent to build the system and then hire the founding recruiter to run it.
