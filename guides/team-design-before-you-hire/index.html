What is team design, and why should startups do it before hiring?

By Elena Stefanopol, fractional Head of Talent, San Francisco · Last updated November 17, 2026

Team design is a 90-minute exercise that lists the work a team must do, rates each person's capability on each piece of work, and shows where coverage is missing. The next hire is then scoped to fill the biggest gap rather than to clone the best performer. It is the fastest way to avoid spending months searching for a candidate who does not exist. In one case documented by San Francisco fractional Head of Talent Elena Stefanopol, a role that had gone unfilled for two months was filled three weeks after the team was mapped.

Key points

  • Most stalled searches are trying to clone a top performer, the "Sam 2.0" problem.
  • List 8 to 15 concrete jobs-to-be-done, rate each team member 1 to 5 on each, and calculate coverage.
  • Write a hire brief with 3 to 5 critical capabilities (not 8+) and a definition of success at 90 days.
  • The full exercise takes 90 minutes; an urgent version takes 30.
  • Revisit the map quarterly on larger teams, since it also guides succession, development and reorganizations.

The Sam 2.0 problem

Elena Stefanopol, a San Francisco-based fractional Head of Talent and the founder of temelia, opened her Team Design series with a familiar story. A support manager, Dave, spent two months looking for "another Sam", his senior specialist with 5+ years of experience. He interviewed dozens of candidates and spent hundreds of hours, with zero hires, while the team drowned in tickets.

Ninety minutes of team mapping showed 85% coverage in customer-facing work, but only 20% in process documentation and 25% in data analysis. Dave did not need another Sam. He needed someone who could build operational systems.

The method

1. Identify jobs-to-be-done

List 8 to 15 concrete activities the team does. "Writes resolution guides for recurring issues" counts. "Communication" does not. Get input from the people doing the work.

2. Map your current team

Rate each person on each job from 1 to 5 and calculate coverage per job. On small teams, a 1 to 3 scale works: 1 = can't do this independently, 2 = can do it independently, 3 = can teach others.

3. Identify your gaps

For each gap, ask: what outcome is blocked? What would become possible if someone owned it?

4. Design for balance

Write a hire brief with 3 to 5 critical capabilities, not 8+. Describe the transformation this hire enables and what success looks like at 90 days.

What happened next

  • Week 1: the new brief changed sourcing. The recruiter searched for operations coordinators and process-improvement specialists instead of support specialists.
  • Week 2: five strong candidates, not dozens of lukewarm ones.
  • Week 3: offer extended and accepted. The hire had 3 years of experience instead of the 5+ the old job description required, but had built documentation systems twice before.

Within six months, the team had documented its 15 most common issues and cut new-hire ramp time from 6 weeks to 3. It traced 60% of escalations to three root causes and gave the product team the data that cut ticket volume by 40%.

Adapting team design to your situation

Situation How to run it
Small team (3 to 5) Whole team present, 15 minutes at a whiteboard, self-assessment on a 1 to 3 scale. Focus on the top 3 to 4 capabilities.
Larger team (10+) Pilot with 5 to 7 people, delegate mapping to team leads, look for capabilities held by only 1 to 2 people at level 4 to 5 (single points of failure). Revisit quarterly.
Remote team Use a shared spreadsheet asynchronously, then debrief the patterns over video.
Urgent hire 30 minutes: list the top 5 to 7 jobs, rate them, name the 1 to 2 biggest gaps, write the brief.

Make it explicit that this is not a performance evaluation. It is a map of what the team covers.

Why this matters for fast-growing startups

Every rushed hire who does not fill the real gap creates recruiting debt: you hire again in 6 to 12 months, spend twice the time and money, and still have the gap. Meanwhile your best people burn out covering work nobody named. Team design is the first step of the system described in how to hire fast without lowering the bar, and it feeds directly into a structured interview process.

Frequently asked questions

What is the Sam 2.0 problem in hiring?

The Sam 2.0 problem is trying to hire a copy of your best performer. It keeps teams stuck because it adds more of the capabilities you already have while the work nobody covers keeps falling through the cracks. Elena Stefanopol named it in her Team Design series.

How long does team design take?

About 90 minutes the first time for a single team. When hiring is urgent, an abbreviated version takes 30 minutes: 15 minutes to list the top 5 to 7 jobs, 10 to rate the team, and 5 to name the one or two biggest gaps.

Should the recruiter be involved in team design?

Yes. A recruiter who understands the team's gaps can source differently. In Elena Stefanopol's example, the recruiter switched searches from customer support specialists to operations and process-improvement profiles and had five strong candidates within a week.
