Founding recruiter vs Head of Talent vs agency: which should your startup use?

By Elena Stefanopol, fractional Head of Talent, San Francisco · Last updated November 17, 2026

Each early-stage hiring model wins in a different situation. Founder-led hiring works for the first 10 to 15 network hires. Agencies work for a few hard-to-fill or executive roles. A founding recruiter works once a hiring system exists for them to run. A full-time Head of Talent works once there is a recruiting team to lead, usually past 100 people. A fractional Head of Talent fills the gap in between: it builds the system so you can scale hiring quickly, then hands it to the team or to the first recruiter.

Key points

  • Founder-led hiring stops scaling once network hires run out, and the bar starts to drift team by team.
  • Agencies charge per hire, commonly 15% to 25% of first-year base salary, and leave no system behind.
  • Many RPOs say seed and Series A startups are too early for them.
  • The founding recruiter is one of the hardest and riskiest early hires: they must be strategic, hands-on and willing to build from zero again.
  • A full-time Head of Talent hired too soon can cost 6 to 12 months of hiring momentum.
  • A fractional Head of Talent builds the system with an end date. temelia's engagements start at $30,000 per month.

Founder-led hiring

Founder-led hiring is the right default for the first team. Those hires come from your network, your investors and friends of friends, and the founders carry the bar personally. It breaks when the plan grows past what founders can personally interview. Nobody owns the process, every team runs it differently, and you end up in talent wars you don't need.

Recruiting agencies

Agencies bring reach and speed on individual searches, which makes them useful for an executive role or a niche specialist. As a startup's main hiring engine they are costly. At a typical 20% fee, 10 engineering hires at $200,000 base cost about $400,000 in fees. The agency also leaves behind no interview process, scorecards or trained managers.

RPOs

Recruitment process outsourcing firms run recruiting for you with their own recruiters and process. They are built for predictable, high-volume hiring. Many will tell a seed or Series A startup that it is too early, and the process they install belongs to them, not to you.

Founding recruiter

The founding recruiter is the first in-house person who owns hiring. Elena Stefanopol, who held this seat herself at Labelbox before becoming its Head of Talent, calls it "the role that makes or breaks a company", one nobody knows how to hire for. You need someone strategic, hands-on and willing to build from zero again, and very few candidates are all three. Founding recruiters do best when they step into an existing system, or have a mandate and the seniority to build one.

Full-time Head of Talent

A full-time Head of Talent is the right hire once there is a recruiting team to build and lead. At 20 to 60 people it is often premature: most experienced Heads of Talent expect to build a team rather than do everything solo. A wrong fit in this seat costs 6 to 12 months of hiring momentum, because you lose the time spent hiring them, the time they spend in the role, and the time spent replacing them.

Fractional Head of Talent

A fractional Head of Talent designs the hiring system on a contract with an end date. That system covers the hiring bar, structured interviews, headcount plan, compensation philosophy, stack and manager training. Then they hand it off, often by hiring the founding recruiter who will run it. This is temelia's model. Elena Stefanopol has used it to scale Labelbox from 50 to 200 people in 12 months and Rundoo from 50 to 100 in 6 months.

How to decide

  • Under 15 people, hiring from your network: stay founder-led, but write down your hiring bar now.
  • 15 to 50 people, network exhausted, a fresh round closed: bring in a fractional Head of Talent to build the system, and use an agency only for one or two specialist roles.
  • System in place, steady volume: hire a founding recruiter to run it. See how to hire your first recruiter.
  • 100+ people with a recruiting team: hire a full-time Head of Talent to lead it.

The data supports bringing in talent expertise early. In Ashby's 2026 State of Startup Hiring report (1,200+ venture-backed startups, 32,000 hires), time to hire at startups under 25 employees dropped by almost 30% when a recruiter was involved.

Frequently asked questions

When should a startup hire its first recruiter?

Hire the first recruiter when hiring volume is steady and a basic system exists for them to run: a hiring bar, an interview process and a headcount plan. Ashby's 2026 data on 1,200+ startups shows that at companies under 25 people, involving a recruiter cuts time to hire by almost 30%.

Are recruiting agencies worth it for early-stage startups?

Agencies are worth it for a few specialized or executive searches. They are expensive as the main hiring engine because they bill per hire, commonly 15% to 25% of first-year base salary, and the process and knowledge leave with them.

Is it too early to hire a Head of Talent at Series A?

Often, yes. Most experienced Heads of Talent expect to build and lead a team, not do everything solo. At Series A, a fractional Head of Talent or a strong founding recruiter working inside a designed system is usually the better fit.

What is the cheapest way to scale hiring at a startup?

Founder-led hiring looks cheapest but costs founder time and produces an inconsistent bar. Over a 10 to 30 hire plan, building a structured system once is usually cheaper than paying agency fees on every hire or making a mis-hire in a senior talent role.
