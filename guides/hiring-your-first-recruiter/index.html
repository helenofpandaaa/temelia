How do you hire your startup's first recruiter?

By Elena Stefanopol, fractional Head of Talent, San Francisco · Last updated November 17, 2026

Hire your first recruiter, often called the founding recruiter, when hiring volume is steady and the founders can no longer run it alone, typically somewhere between 15 and 50 employees. Look for three traits that rarely come together: strategic judgment about what a role should be, hands-on execution in sourcing, screening and closing, and the willingness to build from zero again. Set them up with a hiring bar and an interview process, and give them the mandate to push back on hiring managers. Without those, even a strong recruiter becomes an order-taker.

Key points

  • The founding recruiter is one of the hardest roles to fill and one of the riskiest early hires.
  • The three must-haves are strategic, hands-on and willing to build from zero again. Very few candidates are all three.
  • The best founding recruiters help decide what a role should be and are comfortable pushing back.
  • At startups under 25 people, involving a recruiter cuts time to hire by almost 30% (Ashby, 2026).
  • If no hiring system exists yet, build it first (or have a fractional Head of Talent build it) and then hire the recruiter to run it.

Why the founding recruiter is so hard to hire

Elena Stefanopol calls the founding recruiter "the role that makes or breaks a company, and nobody knows how to hire for it". The person you need must be:

  • Strategic: able to scope roles, plan headcount and advise founders.
  • Hands-on: comfortable sourcing, screening, scheduling and closing every day.
  • Willing to do it again: happy to build from nothing rather than inherit a machine.

Very few recruiters are all three. Experienced leaders often want to manage a team, while strong individual contributors may never have built a process. Elena held this seat herself at Labelbox, where she started as the founding recruiter and later became Head of Talent, growing the company from 50 to 200 people in 12 months.

The biggest mistake: hiring an order-taker

"When the recruiter's job is just to fill the requirement as is and as quickly as possible, without the autonomy needed, you end up making expensive mistakes quickly. The best founding recruiters I know are part of the conversation about what the role should even be, and they're comfortable pushing back."

Elena Stefanopol, interview with She Builds With AI

Give your first recruiter the mandate, explicitly, to challenge role definitions, slow feedback and inconsistent bars.

When to hire your first recruiter

Common triggers:

  • A round closes and the plan calls for 15+ hires in the next year.
  • Founders spend more than a day a week on hiring.
  • Candidates are dropping out because scheduling and feedback are slow.
  • You are paying agency fees on most hires.

The data supports doing it earlier rather than later. Ashby's 2026 State of Startup Hiring report (1,200+ venture-backed startups, 32,000 hires) found that at startups under 25 employees, time to hire drops by almost 30% when a recruiter is involved.

How to interview a founding recruiter

Use a structured process, the same one you will ask them to run:

Competency How to assess it
Role scoping and advising Give them a vague role request and ask them to run a 20-minute intake with you.
0 to 1 process building "Walk me through a hiring process you built from scratch. What broke first?"
Sourcing and closing Live sourcing exercise for one of your real roles, plus a closing story with a competing offer.
Pushback with evidence "Tell me about a time you told a hiring manager their requirements were wrong."
Data and operations How they would set up your ATS, funnel metrics and weekly hiring sync.

Set them up to succeed

Before the founding recruiter starts, have the basics in place: a written hiring bar, an interview process with scorecards, a prioritized headcount plan and a compensation philosophy. If those do not exist, a fractional Head of Talent can build them and hire the recruiter. That is the standard final stage of a temelia engagement.

Frequently asked questions

When should a startup hire its first recruiter?

Hire the first recruiter when hiring is continuous rather than occasional and founders are spending more than a day a week on it, usually between 15 and 50 employees or right after a round that doubles the hiring plan. Ashby's 2026 data shows recruiters cut time to hire by almost 30% at startups under 25 people.

What should I look for in a founding recruiter?

Look for strategic judgment (they help define the role), hands-on execution (they source, screen and close themselves), resilience for 0 to 1 work, and the confidence to push back on founders and hiring managers with evidence. Ask them to walk you through a hiring process they built from scratch.

Should my first recruiter be a contractor or full-time?

Contract recruiters are useful for a burst of roles inside a process that already works. A founding recruiter is a full-time owner of the function. If you have neither a process nor a hiring bar yet, a fractional Head of Talent can build them and then hire the full-time recruiter.
