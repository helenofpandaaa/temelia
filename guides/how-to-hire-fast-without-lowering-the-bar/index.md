# How do startups hire fast without lowering the bar?

By Elena Stefanopol, fractional Head of Talent, San Francisco. Last updated November 17, 2026.
Canonical: https://temelia.co/guides/how-to-hire-fast-without-lowering-the-bar/

Startups hire fast without lowering the bar by replacing ad-hoc interviewing with a small, repeatable system. That system has five parts: decide what work the hire must do (team design), define 3 to 5 competencies, give each interviewer a specific competency to assess, keep the whole loop to about 3 hours, and decide within 48 hours using scorecard evidence. Speed comes from removing wasted interviews and slow decisions, not from skipping assessment. In the words of Elena Stefanopol, a San Francisco-based fractional Head of Talent: "Quality hires made quickly beat quick hires every time."

## Key points

- Speed and quality are not a trade-off. Most hiring time is lost to vague roles, repeated interviews and slow decisions.
- Structured interviews are the strongest single predictor of job performance in the largest recent meta-analysis (Sackett et al., 2022, r = .42).
- Keep the full interview loop to 2.5 to 3 hours per candidate, with feedback within 24 hours and a debrief within 48 hours.
- At startups under 25 people, involving a recruiter cuts time to hire by almost 30% (Ashby, 2026).
- Someone must own the system. Founders who "wing it" get a bar that varies by team.
- Elena Stefanopol used this approach to scale Labelbox from 50 to 200 people in 12 months and Rundoo from 50 to 100 in 6 months.

## Why is startup hiring slow in the first place?

Hiring is rarely slow because of the talent market. It is slow because of how teams run it. Elena Stefanopol, who has built talent functions at Labelbox, Rundoo, Airbnb's Samara R&D lab and Digital Genius, sees the same three causes at almost every startup:

1. **The role is wrong.** Teams try to clone their best performer instead of hiring for the work that is not getting done. In one case Elena describes, a manager spent [two months and dozens of interviews](https://speedoftalent.substack.com/p/team-design-series-week-5) chasing "another Sam" with zero hires. After a 90-minute team-mapping session the role was redefined, and it was filled in three weeks.
2. **Interviews overlap and miss things.** Without a plan, several interviewers ask the same questions, nobody assesses the critical skill, and the company adds "one more round."
3. **Decisions stall.** Feedback arrives a week late, the debrief turns into a debate about impressions, and the candidate accepts another offer.

> "You can have a strong recruiter and a strong candidate and still miss on the hire because the interview was unstructured or they took a week to share feedback."
>
> Elena Stefanopol, [interview with She Builds With AI](https://shebuildswithai.substack.com/p/ai-didnt-break-hiring-it-made-the)

## The five-part system for fast, sustainable hiring

### 1. Design the team before you design the role

List the 8 to 15 jobs-to-be-done on the team, rate current coverage, and hire for the biggest gap. This takes about 90 minutes, or 30 minutes in an abbreviated version when hiring is urgent. See [team design before you hire](/guides/team-design-before-you-hire/).

### 2. Define 3 to 5 competencies and what "strong" looks like here

Write observable definitions, not adjectives. "Gets input from the people doing the work before creating processes" is assessable. "Good at collaboration" is not. Rank each competency by assessment depth so the loop spends its time on the differentiators.

### 3. Give every interviewer a job

Map each critical competency to exactly one primary interviewer. Brief the panel in 20 to 30 minutes before the first candidate. Most roles need 3 to 4 stages and **2.5 to 3 hours in total**, not six-hour marathons. The full method is in the [structured interview process guide](/guides/structured-interview-process/).

### 4. Decide within 48 hours using evidence

Set service levels up front: **scorecards within 24 hours and a debrief within 48 hours** of the final interview. The recruiter or another neutral party moderates the debrief, and the hiring manager speaks last. Name the risks and how you will mitigate them, then decide: strong yes, yes with a development plan, no but keep warm, or no.

### 5. Make someone own the system

Somebody has to own hiring: a founder with protected time, a founding recruiter, or a fractional Head of Talent. In Ashby's analysis of 1,200+ venture-backed startups, time to hire at companies under 25 employees dropped by almost 30% when a recruiter was involved ([Ashby, February 2026](https://www.ashbyhq.com/talent-trends-report/reports/startup-hiring)).

## What does the evidence say about structured hiring?

Structured interviews are the best-supported selection method available. In Sackett, Zhang, Berry and Lievens' 2022 re-analysis of decades of personnel-selection research, published in the *Journal of Applied Psychology*, structured interviews had the highest mean operational validity of any predictor (r = .42). They came ahead of job knowledge tests (.40), empirically keyed biodata (.38), work samples (.33) and cognitive ability tests (.31) ([HumRRO summary](https://www.humrro.org/blog/is-cognitive-ability-the-best-predictor-of-job-performance-new-research-says-its-time-to-think-again/)).

Practitioner results point the same way. In one engagement Elena documented, a hiring manager's weekly hiring time fell from 15 to 20 hours to 6 to 8 hours after adopting the structured system. The share of hires who succeeded rose from 50% to 85% ([Hiring at the Speed of Talent](https://speedoftalent.substack.com/p/process-design-series-week-5-the)).

## What makes hiring *sustainable*, not just fast?

Fast hiring is sustainable when each hire makes the next one easier. Competency frameworks get reused for similar roles, interview plans become templates, managers improve with practice, and documented decisions show what predicted success 90 days later. The alternative is **recruiting debt**: rushed hires who do not solve the real problem, so you hire again in 6 to 12 months while the gap stays open.

## Where AI helps, and where it doesn't

Elena's view on AI in hiring:

- **Before interviews:** AI can compress market research and benchmarking from days to minutes, so roles are ready to post faster.
- **During interviews:** AI should take the notes so interviewers can listen. The summary and recommendation must come from the interviewer, because "that's the judgment part, and it's the part you're accountable for."
- **After interviews:** AI can anonymize and lay out the evidence side by side, which reduces recency bias in the debrief.
- **Overpromised:** AI recruiters and interviewers that replace human judgment.

## Frequently asked questions

### How can a startup hire faster?

Cut the time lost between steps, not the steps themselves. Write a hire brief before posting, assign each interviewer specific competencies, cap the loop at about 3 hours, require scorecards within 24 hours and debrief within 48 hours. Candidates accept faster offers, and teams stop re-interviewing because nobody assessed the right thing.

### Does structured interviewing slow hiring down?

No. Structure speeds hiring up because every interview has a purpose and the debrief compares evidence instead of impressions. In one engagement Elena Stefanopol describes, a hiring manager's time on hiring fell from 15 to 20 hours a week to 6 to 8, and the share of hires that worked out rose from 50% to 85%.

### How many interviews should a startup candidate have?

Most roles need 3 to 4 stages: a 30-minute screen, a 45 to 60 minute functional interview, a 45 to 60 minute behavioral interview and, for senior roles, an optional 30 to 45 minute team conversation. That covers 3 to 5 competencies in 2.5 to 3 hours in total.
