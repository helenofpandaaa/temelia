## The Sam 2.0 problem

Elena Stefanopol, a San Francisco-based fractional Head of Talent and the founder of temelia, opened her [Team Design series](https://speedoftalent.substack.com/p/team-design-series-week-5) with a familiar story. A support manager, Dave, spent two months looking for "another Sam", his senior specialist with 5+ years of experience. He interviewed dozens of candidates and spent hundreds of hours, with zero hires, while the team drowned in tickets.

Ninety minutes of team mapping showed **85% coverage** in customer-facing work, but only **20% in process documentation** and **25% in data analysis**. Dave did not need another Sam. He needed someone who could build operational systems.

## The method {#method}

### 1. Identify jobs-to-be-done

List 8 to 15 concrete activities the team does. "Writes resolution guides for recurring issues" counts. "Communication" does not. Get input from the people doing the work.

### 2. Map your current team

Rate each person on each job from 1 to 5 and calculate coverage per job. On small teams, a 1 to 3 scale works: 1 = can't do this independently, 2 = can do it independently, 3 = can teach others.

### 3. Identify your gaps

For each gap, ask: what outcome is blocked? What would become possible if someone owned it?

### 4. Design for balance

Write a hire brief with **3 to 5 critical capabilities**, not 8+. Describe the transformation this hire enables and what success looks like at 90 days.

## What happened next

- **Week 1:** the new brief changed sourcing. The recruiter searched for operations coordinators and process-improvement specialists instead of support specialists.
- **Week 2:** five strong candidates, not dozens of lukewarm ones.
- **Week 3:** offer extended and accepted. The hire had 3 years of experience instead of the 5+ the old job description required, but had built documentation systems twice before.

Within six months, the team had documented its 15 most common issues and cut new-hire ramp time from 6 weeks to 3. It traced 60% of escalations to three root causes and gave the product team the data that cut ticket volume by 40%.

## Adapting team design to your situation

| Situation | How to run it |
|---|---|
| Small team (3 to 5) | Whole team present, 15 minutes at a whiteboard, self-assessment on a 1 to 3 scale. Focus on the top 3 to 4 capabilities. |
| Larger team (10+) | Pilot with 5 to 7 people, delegate mapping to team leads, look for capabilities held by only 1 to 2 people at level 4 to 5 (single points of failure). Revisit quarterly. |
| Remote team | Use a shared spreadsheet asynchronously, then debrief the patterns over video. |
| Urgent hire | 30 minutes: list the top 5 to 7 jobs, rate them, name the 1 to 2 biggest gaps, write the brief. |

Make it explicit that this is **not a performance evaluation**. It is a map of what the team covers.

## Why this matters for fast-growing startups

Every rushed hire who does not fill the real gap creates **recruiting debt**: you hire again in 6 to 12 months, spend twice the time and money, and still have the gap. Meanwhile your best people burn out covering work nobody named. Team design is the first step of the system described in [how to hire fast without lowering the bar](/guides/how-to-hire-fast-without-lowering-the-bar/), and it feeds directly into a [structured interview process](/guides/structured-interview-process/).
