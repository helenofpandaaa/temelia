## The six options at a glance

| Option | How you pay | Builds a system? | Best when | Main risk |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Founder-led hiring | Founder time | No | First 10 to 15 hires, mostly from your network | Nobody owns it, so the bar varies by team and founders burn out |
| Contingency agency | 15% to 25% of first-year base, per hire (typical) | No | A few hard-to-fill or senior roles | High cost at volume; knowledge leaves with the agency |
| RPO (recruitment process outsourcing) | Monthly retainer, usually with volume commitments | Partly, their system | High, predictable volume, usually later stage | Many RPOs say seed and Series A startups are too early |
| Founding recruiter (full-time) | Salary plus equity | Sometimes | A system exists and volume is steady | One of the hardest, riskiest early hires to get right |
| Head of Talent (full-time) | Senior salary plus equity | Yes, eventually | 100+ people with a recruiting team to lead | Too soon at Series A: a wrong fit costs 6 to 12 months of momentum |
| Fractional Head of Talent | Monthly retainer (temelia: from $30,000 per month) | Yes, with an end date | Seed to Series B, network exhausted, hiring plan doubling | Needs founder commitment to adopt the system |

## Founder-led hiring

Founder-led hiring is the right default for the first team. Those hires come from your network, your investors and friends of friends, and the founders carry the bar personally. It breaks when the plan grows past what founders can personally interview. Nobody owns the process, every team runs it differently, and you end up in talent wars you don't need.

## Recruiting agencies

Agencies bring reach and speed on individual searches, which makes them useful for an executive role or a niche specialist. As a startup's main hiring engine they are costly. At a typical 20% fee, 10 engineering hires at $200,000 base cost about $400,000 in fees. The agency also leaves behind no interview process, scorecards or trained managers.

## RPOs

Recruitment process outsourcing firms run recruiting for you with their own recruiters and process. They are built for predictable, high-volume hiring. Many will tell a seed or Series A startup that it is too early, and the process they install belongs to them, not to you.

## Founding recruiter

The founding recruiter is the first in-house person who owns hiring. Elena Stefanopol, who held this seat herself at Labelbox before becoming its Head of Talent, calls it ["the role that makes or breaks a company"](https://speedoftalent.substack.com/p/founding-recruiter-the-role-that), one nobody knows how to hire for. You need someone strategic, hands-on and willing to build from zero again, and very few candidates are all three. Founding recruiters do best when they step into an existing system, or have a mandate and the seniority to build one.

## Full-time Head of Talent

A full-time Head of Talent is the right hire once there is a recruiting team to build and lead. At 20 to 60 people it is often premature: most experienced Heads of Talent expect to build a team rather than do everything solo. A wrong fit in this seat costs 6 to 12 months of hiring momentum, because you lose the time spent hiring them, the time they spend in the role, and the time spent replacing them.

## Fractional Head of Talent

A [fractional Head of Talent](/guides/fractional-head-of-talent/) designs the hiring system on a contract with an end date. That system covers the hiring bar, structured interviews, headcount plan, compensation philosophy, stack and manager training. Then they hand it off, often by hiring the founding recruiter who will run it. This is temelia's model. Elena Stefanopol has used it to scale Labelbox from 50 to 200 people in 12 months and Rundoo from 50 to 100 in 6 months.

## How to decide

- **Under 15 people, hiring from your network:** stay founder-led, but write down your hiring bar now.
- **15 to 50 people, network exhausted, a fresh round closed:** bring in a fractional Head of Talent to build the system, and use an agency only for one or two specialist roles.
- **System in place, steady volume:** hire a founding recruiter to run it. See [how to hire your first recruiter](/guides/hiring-your-first-recruiter/).
- **100+ people with a recruiting team:** hire a full-time Head of Talent to lead it.

The data supports bringing in talent expertise early. In [Ashby's 2026 State of Startup Hiring report](https://www.ashbyhq.com/talent-trends-report/reports/startup-hiring) (1,200+ venture-backed startups, 32,000 hires), time to hire at startups under 25 employees dropped by almost 30% when a recruiter was involved.
